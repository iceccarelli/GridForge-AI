# Calibration publication gates (migration 0056): operator runbook

Status: **written, not applied** to any database. The ledger holds 0 real observations. Nothing here is an instruction to apply it; whether and when is the owner's decision.

## What 0056 does

`supabase/migrations/0056_calibration_publication_gates.sql` adds four gates an observation clears *before it is recorded* (a trigger on `project_observations`, so it binds every way a row arrives) and *again before it is published* (the final `calibration_ledger_rows` view, which now holds three more conditions than 0052's):

- **D1 completion milestone.** `project_completion_milestones`: an append-only record that a project reached `energised` on a date (not before 2000, not in the future), backed by a *verified* file of that project, attested by an owner or editor of its organisation. The record is **bound to the organisation it was attested under** (derived from the project's hall, never supplied): after a project is moved to another organisation the old record is absent for it and the new organisation attests afresh (the refusal says so). A wrong date is corrected by an **owner** with a later record that supersedes the first (`supersedes_id`; nothing is edited; the gate and the view read the record nothing supersedes). A production observation is refused without a standing milestone, when dated before it, or when a supplier record of the project shows delivery still in flight: a line whose latest unrejected record has no energisation date on or before the observed date is in flight if *any* unrejected record of its chain gives any date, so a cost-only correction cannot make it look delivered (to clear it, a later record states the energisation date). Error `GF016`.
- **D3 consent.** `calibration_consents`: an append-only log per *organisation* of a grant or withdrawal that names the scope (`aggregate_residual_statistics`), the aggregation floors (at least 10 observations, 3 organisations) and the version and SHA-256 of the consent text. Only an active owner can write it. A production observation needs its project attached to a hall of an organisation whose latest consent is a grant; the organisation is derived from that attachment, never supplied. The insert gate takes the consent writer's advisory lock before it reads the consent, so a withdrawal that arrives while an observation insert is open waits for it and tombstones that row too. Errors `GF015`, `GF016`.
- **D5 withdrawal.** A withdrawal writes a tombstone (the SHA-256 of each observation id, nothing else) for every observation recorded under the organisation, marks released snapshots that contained them as superseded, and the view excludes them for ever. The view also requires that no withdrawal followed the consent a row was recorded under, whatever was granted since. Error `GF017` for a tombstoned observation.
- **R4 release registry.** `calibration_release_snapshots` refuses a release that is not a safe group (10 observations, 3 organisations, none above 40%), comes within 30 days of the previous one *as the registry's own clock recorded them* (the caller's `released_at` is held to within a day of that clock: no back-dating, no forward-dating; `GF018`), or differs from any earlier one by less than a safe group. The numbers are `gf_calibration_release_policy()` and `gridforge/calibration/release.py`; a test compares them.

## What applying it does to the site, plainly

From the moment it is applied, **every production calibration submission fails closed** (HTTP 409 from `POST /api/projects/[token]/observations`) until, for that project: it is attached to a hall of an organisation, **that organisation's owner has recorded a consent**, and **the project's completion milestone has been attested**. **The site has no page for either.** Nothing in `app/` or `lib/` calls `gf_record_calibration_consent`, `gf_withdraw_calibration_consent` or `gf_attest_completion_milestone`; they are `service_role`-only functions, so today an operator must call them (PostgREST `rpc`, or SQL as the service role). The customer sees a sentence naming what is missing (`lib/calibration-gate-errors.ts`) that says there is no page yet and points to /contact. Preview and test deployments are unaffected (their rows are not `production`).

## Order of operations

1. **Decide.** Applying changes behaviour customers can see (above) and cannot be undone except by a new migration. The application cannot yet meet the gates by itself.
2. **Prerequisites.** `scripts/apply-migration.py` gate 2 refuses 0056 unless 0055 (the previous file) is in the remote history and nothing higher is. `lib/capability-registry.ts` records 0046-0048 and 0049-0052 as applied on 2026-11-07 (`docs/EXECUTION_STATE.md` itself stops at 0045); the headers of 0053, 0054 and 0055 say written, not applied, so each is its own operator decision first. Read the live history before relying on either record.
3. **Backup gate.** `python3 scripts/export-prod-data.py`, then `python3 scripts/verify-backup-restore.py` (it rehearses pending migrations on the restored rows; it must say RESTORE VERIFIED), then `python3 scripts/apply-migration.py 0056` as a dry run: gate 3 wants an export under `~/gf-backups` newer than 2 hours whose per-table counts equal the live ones.
4. **Apply** with `--yes`. The file sets `lock_timeout = '5s'` itself (`SET LOCAL`, effective because the applier submits the file as one query, i.e. one transaction), and each statement that takes an ACCESS EXCLUSIVE lock on a table other sessions use sets it again for itself: a reader that holds `project_observations` or `project_events` open makes the apply **fail after five seconds** instead of queueing behind it and blocking everyone else; re-run it when the table is quiet. The column, the trigger and the event-type check are only touched when something is missing, so a **re-apply takes none of those locks**. (`CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW` does lock the view: a query reading `calibration_ledger_rows` at that moment meets the same timeout.) `tests/test_calibration_publication_gates.py` applies it against a held-open reader, statement by statement and as one transaction.
5. **Verify** (read-only; `tests/test_calibration_final_gate.py` and `tests/test_calibration_publication_gates.py` assert the same on a scratch database):
   - Q1 `select to_regclass('public.calibration_consents'), to_regclass('public.project_completion_milestones'), to_regclass('public.calibration_tombstones'), to_regclass('public.calibration_release_snapshots'), to_regclass('public.calibration_snapshot_supersessions');` all non-null.
   - Q2 `select pg_get_viewdef('public.calibration_ledger_rows'::regclass, true);` names `calibration_consents`, `project_completion_milestones` and `calibration_tombstones`.
   - Q3 `select tgname, tgenabled from pg_trigger where tgname in ('project_observations_publication_gates','calibration_consents_guard','calibration_consents_withdrawn','project_completion_milestones_guard','calibration_release_snapshots_guard');` five rows, all `O`.
   - Q4 `select (select count(*) from project_observations), (select count(*) from project_observations where organisation_id is not null), (select count(*) from calibration_ledger_rows);` the first equals the pre-apply count, the others are 0.
   - Q5 `select public.gf_calibration_release_policy();` is `min_cell_observations 10, min_cell_organisations 3, max_organisation_share_permille 400, release_step_observations 10, release_step_organisations 3, min_release_interval_days 30`.
   - Q6 privileges, which a Supabase project's **default privileges** would otherwise leave open (they grant ALL on every new public table to `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role`; the migration revokes before it grants): `select table_name, grantee, privilege_type from information_schema.role_table_grants where table_schema = 'public' and table_name in ('calibration_consents','calibration_release_snapshots','project_completion_milestones','calibration_tombstones','calibration_snapshot_supersessions','calibration_ledger_rows') and grantee in ('anon','authenticated','PUBLIC','service_role');` shows **no** row for `anon`, `authenticated` or `PUBLIC`, and for `service_role` only `SELECT` on the tombstones, the supersessions and the view, and `SELECT` plus `INSERT` on the consents, the release snapshots and the milestones (the `gf_*` functions write those as the caller; the tombstones and supersessions are written by the SECURITY DEFINER trigger alone). A forged `INSERT` into `calibration_tombstones` as `service_role` must fail with `permission denied`. The two identity sequences (`calibration_consents`, `calibration_release_snapshots`) have no privilege for any API role.
6. **Make a project submittable (per organisation, by hand until there is a page).** The owner's e-mail must be an active owner in `organisation_members`. Consent: `gf_record_calibration_consent(p)` with `organisation_id, publish_scope, min_aggregate_observations (>=10), min_aggregate_organisations (>=3), consent_text_version, consent_text_sha256, document_sha256, recorded_by`. Milestone: `gf_attest_completion_milestone(p, p_event)` with `project_id, milestone 'energised', reached_on (2000-01-01 .. today), evidence_id (verified file of that project), basis, attested_by (owner or editor)`, and a `completion_milestone_attested` history event. To **correct a wrong date**, the same call by an **owner** with `supersedes_id` set to the standing record; to move a project to **another organisation**, detach it (owner of the first) and attach it (editor of the second), after which the second organisation attests the milestone itself.
7. **Admin export.** `GET /api/admin/calibration-export` also returns `tombstones` (hashes). `gridforge calibrate import` **records them** in the side file beside the private ledger (`calibration.local.tombstones.json`, `GRIDFORGE_CALIBRATION_TOMBSTONES` moves it) before it looks at the rows, and from then on every read of the ledger leaves those observations out and an import of an export that holds one is refused. `gridforge calibrate withdraw <64-hex hash>...` records hashes by hand (`calibrate where` shows the count). **Nothing reads the network**: a withdrawal reaches the private ledger when an operator imports an export that lists it or runs `withdraw`, and not before; until then the ledger keeps counting what it holds, and an export file saved before the withdrawal is refused only once its tombstones are recorded here. After a withdrawal in the database, import a fresh export (or run `withdraw`) promptly.

## What the release proof (R4) proves, and what it does not

`gridforge/calibration/release.py` rule 3 holds a release to this, exactly (integer arithmetic, over a basis kept per *atom*, a class of observations that lie in exactly the same published sets): no atom smaller than a safe group has a sum that can be read off, and **no coalition of at most M - 1 = 2 organisations, each knowing exactly its own observations, can isolate one observation of another organisation** from any combination of the published sums, across the whole history. The attacker sees every sum exactly. It replaces a test that only asked whether one small group was recoverable on its own (a sum over two small groups passed it; the review's sweep found an organisation with only its own data isolating another's observation in 4 of 150 seeds, and the same sweep now finds none). The stronger model the review proposed (the attacker knows every other small atom) was built and refuses every release for ever after the first withdrawal, so it is not what runs; the header of `release.py` says why. **Not defended**, stated there and repeated here:

- **A coalition of three or more organisations** that between them own all but one observation of a safe group.
- **Tenant splitting and Sybil organisations.** M and the 40% cap count organisations as the registry knows them; one address can create up to twenty. **Operational mitigation, the operator's until verified-organisation identity exists:** treat organisations created from one address or one e-mail domain (public webmail domains such as gmail.com counted per address, not per domain) as ONE organisation for M and for the 40% cap, by giving all of them the same organisation key before the release layer sees them (`Contribution.organisation`). **The rows do not carry an owner address**: `gf_calibration_publication_inputs()` returns hashes and the consent floors only, so no grouping by owner domain is done in code and none is guessed. To automate it, the inputs function would have to return a hash of the grouping key and `Contribution` would use it; that is a design decision, not done here.
- **A consent given for a stranger's project.** The holder of a project's link token who is an owner (or editor, for the attachment) of an organisation can attach the project to that organisation's hall and have the owner consent for it; the registry does not check the buyer against the organisation (0050). Check, before a project's observations are published, that the organisation is the project's real owner.
- **Figures that are not unweighted sums.** The proof treats each published figure as the sum (or the mean over a known count) over its observation set. A mean of site means weights observations of one atom differently and needs the weights in the incidence matrix. Rounding is not modelled.
- **A limit, not a pass:** more than 1000 atoms (`MAX_PROOF_ATOMS`) and the proof gives up, refusing the release. The previous limit was 300 *published sets*, which refused every release after a long monthly history (the 20-year simulation in `tests/test_release_differencing.py` keeps releasing).

## Open items

- **R4 is not wired.** `propose_release` is not called by the API's `public_report` (`gridforge/api/server.py` still recomputes it on every request), and nothing registers a snapshot. **No aggregate may be published, and no real observation put in the ledger, until it is** (`publication.py`, HARD PRECONDITION).
- **Pre-0056 rows are not back-filled.** They have no organisation or consent, so they are not in the view, and none is invented for them.
- **No page** to record or withdraw consent, or to attest or correct the milestone. The admin checklist (`/admin/reviews`) reads the six gates and the record's shape, not these three conditions: the view alone applies them.
- **The public residual report does not read this ledger.** `GET /v1/calibration/residuals` (`store.py`, `records.py`, `residuals.py`) reads its own record store (`calibration.records.local.jsonl`), which neither `gridforge calibrate import` nor 0056 writes, and it has no consent or tombstone concept: a withdrawal does not reach it, and nothing from the 0056 view flows into it today. If records are ever fed to it from the ledger, the tombstone filter has to be added there first. The bundled ledger (`data/calibration.json`) has no tombstones either: a published row is withdrawn by a commit.
- **Unchecked by the database:** the consent text and signed document (digests the owner typed), who is an owner (the registry's say-so), the milestone date (the attester's declaration; an owner can correct it).
- **Replay order.** Re-running 0038, 0046-0048 or 0052 after 0056 puts an older, weaker view back; `tests/test_calibration_final_gate.py` reports it and 0056 restores it. The calibration export reads the view in one request, which a hosted PostgREST caps (1000 rows by default): not a problem at 0 rows, a truncation to fix before there are more.
